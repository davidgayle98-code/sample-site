What we train

A Well-Rounded Curriculum

Our roots are in Isshinryu karate, developed by Master Tatsuo Shimabuku on Okinawa. Our training is directed at self-defense and personal growth, not sport or recreation. To that base, Grandmaster Cooling and his senior black belts added more.

Isshin-Ryu KarateThe foundation

A hybrid of the two styles Master Shimabuku studied, Shorin-ryu and Goju-ryu, plus classical Okinawan weaponry.

KobudoTraditional Okinawan weapons

Bo, sai, tuifa and more, the weapons Shimabuku himself studied under Taira Shinken.

KendoArt of the sword

Traditional Japanese sword training, part of the wider curriculum available to members.

Chin-na & KyushoJoint locks and pressure points

Control techniques, along with pain and leverage compliance, for practical self-defense.

SMP ArnisStick and knife

Filipino stick and knife work, plus ground fighting, for a full understanding of self-defense.

Chi-Ryu Jiujitsu & Shikyori BudoSystems built by our own masters

Chi-Ryu, the "Way of Energy Through Gentle Technique," from Masters Latimer, Costanzo and McDonald. Shikyori Budo, Master Ron Tyree's blend of Muay Thai and Jujitsu.

Why we exist

Our Goals

  1. Provide one of the best self-defense and traditional training programs available.
  2. Provide and maintain standards for the attainment of black belt status.
  3. Foster a sense of family and community among members.
  4. Organize trainings, competitions, and special classes so members can learn and grow.
  5. Keep the costs of participation as low as possible.

Evolve and grow in the martial arts community through research and training in the family atmosphere founded by Grandmaster Toby Cooling in 1971.

Mission

Develop martial artists through opportunities to participate and train in various arts and provide exposure to top-level instructors through our affiliations.

The men behind the style

Our Lineage

Founder of Isshin-Ryu Karate

Tatsuo Shimabuku

1908 – 1975 · Chan, Okinawa

Born Shinkichi Shimabuku, he began karate as a boy under his uncle, then walked hours each day to study Shorin-ryu with Chotoku Kyan. He went on to learn from Chojun Miyagi of Goju-ryu, the legendary fighter Choki Motobu, and kobudo master Taira Shinken.

On January 15, 1956, after nine years of experimenting, he named his new style Isshin-Ryu. Many senior instructors on Okinawa disagreed, and the style went unrecognized for years, but he persevered.

Read the full story

During and after World War II he and his family lived in Japan. He returned to Okinawa in 1947 and began teaching karate. At 39 he took the name Tatsuo, "Dragon Man," following the Okinawan custom of taking a karate name.

Through the 1950s and '60s Master Shimabuku was hired to train US servicemen stationed on Okinawa, and Isshin-Ryu grew tremendously across the United States as a result. He continued to experiment and challenge the traditional concepts of karate until he retired from active teaching in 1971.

Those fortunate Americans who trained with him will tell you of a man who enjoyed sake and cigarettes and loved to joke, and of a phenomenal martial artist who struck awe in his students.

Founder of the Order

Walter E. "Toby" Cooling

1944 – 2022 · Chesapeake City, Maryland

Toby Cooling started in Judo at sixteen and began Isshin-Ryu at twenty under Sensei Tom Lewis in Salisbury, MD. He went on to train under Master Don Nagle, one of Master Shimabuku's first American students, and earned his shodan in 1965.

In December 1969 he flew to Okinawa carrying a letter of introduction to train with the founder himself. Before he left, Master Shimabuku promoted him to roku-dan, 6th degree black belt.

"…go back to the States and teach good Isshin-Ryu for me, make happy family."Master Shimabuku to Toby Cooling. The Order of Isshin-Ryu was founded on January 15, 1971 to keep that promise.
Read the full story

Back home he started his own real estate firm and later owned and operated Jayco Liquors from 1975 to 1989. He was promoted to hachi-dan in 1986 and ku-dan, 9th degree, on June 14, 1992 by Masters Don Nagle and Harold Long. In 1995 the World Head of Family Sokeship Council recognized him as one of the heads of the Isshin-Ryu family worldwide.

He was inducted into the Isshinryu Hall of Fame in 1988 and received its Spirit of Isshinryu award in 1991. He became proficient in chin-na and kyusho, taught municipal, county, state and federal law enforcement agencies, served as a Cecil County police officer, and was a reserve deputy for the Douglas County, Nevada Sheriff's Department.

Grandmaster Cooling passed away on February 12, 2022. He wanted the Order to be a family first and an organization second. He expected the very best from his students in martial arts and in life, and above all he believed that when one gave his word, he should keep it at all costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What type of martial art is Order of Isshin-Ryu Karate?
It has its roots in Isshinryu Karate (one heart way), an Okinawan martial art. Our training is directed specifically at self-defense and personal growth, not sport or recreation. To that base we add ground fighting, Chin-na (joint locks), Kyusho (pressure points), Arnis (stick and knife), and Pain/Leverage Compliance techniques. We keep investigating and learning from many arts so our students have a well-rounded understanding of self-defense.
How do you advance in the Order of Isshin-Ryu?
When a student shows they have reached a set level of proficiency, a new colored belt or a stripe is awarded. Evaluations are held periodically, when the instructor feels the student is ready, not on a set time schedule.
How long does it take to reach black belt?
It is purely individual and depends on how much time you give to practice. On average it takes about 4 months of steady training to reach the next level, and anywhere from 3 to 6 years in total to earn a black belt. Progression is not the belt around your waist, it is the knowledge you have and your ability to apply it. All students meet the same standards. Belts are not given, they are earned.
Can I train at other OI dojos?
Yes. As a fraternal organization, members of an OI dojo may train at any other member school, provided they discuss it first with their own instructor.
Can I train in another style at the same time?
Every martial system has its own way of doing things, and learning two arts at once is confusing for beginners. We prefer you choose one and earn your black belt, then diversify. We encourage our black belts to cross-train in other arts.
Are there special rules or etiquette?
Your instructor will give you a copy of the dojo rules and talk with you about etiquette. Some rules may seem strange at first, but they have been found to create the best atmosphere for learning. Karate begins and ends with courtesy.
Do you take part in tournaments?
We hold a shiai (pronounced she-eye) for our member schools every few months. This traditional Okinawan event brings all students together to meet, train, compete, and for instructors to award promotions. We also encourage members to attend outside events.
